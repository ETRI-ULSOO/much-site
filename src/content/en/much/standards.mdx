## Why a Standard Was Needed

Even when recording the same artifact digitally, institutions differed in their equipment, lighting conditions, resolution, and file formats. As a result, quality varied depending on which institution produced the data, and material from one institution was hard to use as-is at another. This is why fixing the procedure and quality criteria for producing data in a document mattered as much as building the platform itself.

## Enacted Standard

### Guidelines for Quality Assurance in Digital Data Generation of Movable Cultural Heritage

This was enacted as a Telecommunications Technology Association (TTA) standard on 5 December 2025. Its standard number is TTAK.KO-10.1621. It sets out the procedure and quality requirements to follow when creating digital data for movable cultural heritage — that is, cultural heritage that can be relocated.

The process leading to enactment was as follows. It was adopted as a new project of the Metaverse Project Group (PG610) on 28 August 2024 (project number 2024-3100), and after a process of refining the draft standard, it was enacted in December of the following year.

- The full text is available from the [TTA Standardization Committee document search](https://committee.tta.or.kr/data/standard_view.jsp?rn1=Y&standard_no=TTAK.KO-10.1621&pk_num=TTAK.KO-10.1621&nowSu=1&rn=1) (checked 14 September 2026). The Korean title is 「동산 문화유산 디지털 데이터 생성 품질 유지를 위한 제작 지침」.

### Immersive Mixed Reality Technology Forum Standard (August 2024)

This is a forum standard enacted on 13 August 2024, ahead of the TTA standard, by the Interoperability Standards Subcommittee of the Immersive Mixed Reality Technology Forum. It bears the same title as the TTA standard, and its document number is IITF_02.0015/R0. The content refined here became the basis for the TTA standard the following year.

## Guidelines

### Cultural Heritage Digital Asset Standard Guidelines 2024

This book sets out overall standards for turning cultural heritage into digital assets, published on 23 December 2024 (ISBN 979-11-969760-1-9). It covers how to classify data types, the metadata that must be recorded for each item, and file formats and compression methods. Its metadata fields were designed to mesh with other institutions' systems by drawing on the CIDOC Conceptual Reference Model (CIDOC CRM), the European cultural heritage metadata standard CARARE, Dublin Core, and Schema.org, the structured vocabulary used for web search.

The National Museum of Korea adopted this guideline as the delivery specification for its gigapixel photography and 3D data acquisition contracts — meaning the document is actually used as a standard in real procurement.

### RTI Data Acquisition Guideline

Organizes the photography procedure for RTI, a method that captures surface texture by shooting under light from many changing directions. It covers lighting angles, the number of shots, and calibration methods.

### 3D Data Acquisition Guideline

Organizes the procedure for recording an artifact's three-dimensional shape. It covers choosing a scanning method, resolution criteria, and post-processing steps.

## Classification System and Relationship Definitions

Alongside the guidelines, the project produced the Cultural Heritage Classification, Tagging, and Relationship Definition document, which defines how artifacts are categorized and how they relate to one another. Version 2.0 covered 1,192 instances and 5,228 items; version 3.0 expanded this to 1,714 instances and 5,926 items.

## International Standardization in Progress

Building on the content enacted as a domestic standard, the project is pursuing standardization with the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). This page will be updated as progress is confirmed.
